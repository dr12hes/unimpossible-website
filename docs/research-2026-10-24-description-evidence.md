# Homepage description: evidence review

Reviewed 24 October 2026. Research and editorial review only; no live changes.

## Verdict

The live wording is a reasonable concise description, but **its superiority over the previous wording is unproven**. Publishing, verification and undo establish that our system works; they do not establish better search results. Treat this publication as a monitored editorial hypothesis, not a validated SEO gain.

Current: “Norfolk-based engineers helping businesses explore AI and build practical software. Book a free 30-minute conversation with UNimpossible.”

Previous: “UNimpossible is a small team of engineers in Norfolk. We help businesses understand what AI can do for them, then we build it. Fast. Free 30-minute conversation; free one-hour session for Norfolk Chambers of Commerce members.”

## Primary-source findings

Google recommends accurate, page-specific summaries with useful details. It may use the description, but often generates query-dependent snippets from page content. It sets no fixed description character limit; displayed snippets may be shortened for the device. Consequently, shorter wording alone is not evidence of better performance. Both versions broadly meet the descriptive guidance. [Google: snippets and descriptions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)

Google's AI-search guidance continues to prioritise established SEO, useful original content and technical discoverability. It does not provide evidence that this description rewrite will earn AI citations. Eligibility does not guarantee inclusion. These Google sources also cannot establish effects in ChatGPT or Claude. [Google: generative AI search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)

The homepage supports the Norfolk location, engineering services, AI focus and free 30-minute conversation. It also advertises a free one-hour session for Norfolk Chambers members. The rewrite loses that distinctive offer; this is a genuine editorial tradeoff, not an automatic improvement. The fetched page was labelled crawled three days ago, so this review is corroboration of the offer, not a fresh production HTML verification. [UNimpossible homepage](https://unimpossible.co.uk/)

## Evidence limitations and recommendation

- Confidence in factual fit: reasonably high, subject to the cached-page limitation. Confidence in increased clicks or AI referrals: low; no measured evidence yet.
- No usable pre-change search baseline was reported when imports began. We cannot reconstruct a controlled comparison merely by starting tracking now.
- The dashboard's seven-day before/after view is observational. Site-wide totals, changing query mix, rankings, migration, recrawling, seasonality and other edits can move results. Seven days is a display window, not a causal test or a universal minimum sample.
- AI referral requests are not unique people, citations or total AI visibility. Many visits provide no identifying referrer. Google may not display the supplied description.
- Leave the approved wording stable while observations accumulate. Do not rewrite it again solely to satisfy a length rule. Record the lost Chambers detail for the next deliberate editorial review.
- Desired outcome: more relevant enquiries. Search clicks and CTR are intermediate observations; this slice does not establish enquiry attribution. A future page/query-level comparison should check impressions and ranking/context before interpreting CTR. Sparse data should remain “insufficient evidence”, not “no impact”.

## Current implementation versus proposed review contract

The [implemented change-and-traffic specification](plan-2026-10-24-change-impact.md) provides dated events, exact-origin filtering, source-aware seven-day comparisons and explicit restrictions against claiming causation. Prior work reports approval, publish, verify and undo tracking. This review does not independently re-test those production operations.

The following is **proposed acceptance guidance**, not a claim that the dashboard already enforces it. Each recommendation should carry one short evidence record:

1. **Finding:** the observed issue, affected URL and existing text; distinguish missing information from an optional rewrite.
2. **Rationale and source:** why the change may help, primary-source link and review date, plus the actual page facts supporting the wording.
3. **Confidence and tradeoff:** separate confidence in fixing a technical defect from confidence in business benefit; identify information removed or claims introduced.
4. **Measurement:** intended outcome, available baseline, metric and coverage limits; choose a review window appropriate to available traffic, without promising significance by a fixed date.
5. **Review and approval:** reviewer checks factual accuracy, page relevance and evidence before user approval; retain the reviewed version with the existing timeline.
6. **After publishing:** verify the exact live change, keep undo available, and report observations as pending, insufficient evidence, or directional movement—not proven causation.

This can start as a compact review note attached to each suggestion. It needs no new testing framework, automatic optimisation loop or separate research system.
